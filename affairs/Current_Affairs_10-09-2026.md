#### 2. [FCNR(B) Deposits, Currency Risk, RBI Swap, Banks]
*   **What happened:** Financial analysts have shed light on the structural mechanics of Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) [FCNR(B)] deposits, specifically examining how currency and interest rate risks are distributed between the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and commercial banking institutions.
*   **Key Background & Context:** When commercial banks mobilize FCNR(B) dollar deposits from non-resident Indians (NRIs), they face severe exchange rate risks if the Indian Rupee depreciates significantly against the US dollar. To mitigate systemic instability, the RBI often utilizes a specialized foreign exchange swap mechanism that effectively shields commercial banks from currency risks pertaining to the principal amount. However, participating banks are still required to independently manage regular dollar interest payments, exposing them to financial vulnerability if the domestic currency weakens sharply over the deposit tenure.
*   **Relevant Numbers & Stats:** The financial mechanism involves multi-billion dollar NRI deposit flows and specialized **RBI foreign exchange swap windows** designed to manage foreign exchange reserves.
*   **Organizations & Personalities Involved:** Reserve Bank of India (RBI), scheduled commercial banks, and Non-Resident Indians (NRIs).
*   **Exam Relevance:** Crucial for banking examinations (IBPS, SBI, RBI Grade B) and UPSC Economy sections focusing on monetary policy, foreign exchange reserves, and banking risk management.
