    **UPI MDR: Who will pay, who won’t, and what the new rules mean**
    The government has clarified that the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) will remain free for consumers and small merchants, with potential Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) charges applicable only to select transactions above a certain threshold. This announcement underscores the government's intent to keep UPI an inclusive and accessible platform for digital transactions in the country. MDR is the fee that merchants pay to banks for each credit or debit card transaction. The issue of imposing MDR on UPI transactions has been a subject of debate previously, especially when it comes to ensuring a viable revenue model for service providers. This move by the government is crucial for promoting financial inclusion and easing the burden on small businesses, allowing them to continue accepting digital payments without additional costs. The decision reflects the direction of India's digital economy and efforts to balance interests across various stakeholders—consumers, merchants, and payment service providers. This is important for competitive exams as it highlights the government's Digital India initiative, financial technology policies, and their impact on the economy.
